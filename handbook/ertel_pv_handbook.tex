\section{Ertel PV in Height Coordinates}

Ertel's potential vorticity \citep{ertel1942} in height coordinates $(x,y,z)$:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:epv_z}
\mathrm{EPV} = \frac{1}{\rho}\,(\vec{\omega}_a \cdot \nabla\theta)
\end{equation}

where $\vec{\omega}_a = \nabla \times \vec{u} + 2\vec{\Omega}$ (absolute
vorticity), $\theta = T(p_0/p)^{R_d/c_p}$ (dry potential temperature,
$p_0 = 1000$\,hPa).

Horizontal derivatives use spectrally accurate spherical harmonic transforms
via \texttt{pvtend.sh\_ops}, eliminating polar singularities.

\section{Sigma-Coordinate Formulation}\label{sec:sigma}

Terrain-following sigma: $\sigma = p/p_s$ where $p_s(x,y)$ is surface
pressure [Pa].  Mimics MPAS-Atmosphere's native vertical coordinate.

\textbf{Important distinction:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $p_0 = 100{,}000$\,Pa = constant --- thermodynamic reference for $\theta$
  \item $p_s(x,y)$ = varying 2-D field --- surface pressure for $\sigma = p/p_s$
\end{itemize}

These are separate quantities; $p_0$ never appears in the sigma PV formula.

Coordinate transformation $\partial/\partial p \to (1/p_s)\partial/\partial\sigma$:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:epv_sigma}
\boxed{\mathrm{PV}_\sigma = -\frac{g}{p_s}\left[
  \frac{\partial v}{\partial\sigma}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial x}\Big|_\sigma
  - \frac{\partial u}{\partial\sigma}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial y}\Big|_\sigma
  + (f + \zeta_\sigma)\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\sigma}
\right]}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Analytic equivalence:} Ertel PV is coordinate-invariant:
$\mathrm{PV}_\sigma(x,y,\sigma) = \mathrm{PV}_p(x,y,p=\sigma p_s)$.

\textbf{Simplified} form: $\mathrm{PV}_{\sigma,\mathrm{simple}} =
  -(g/p_s)\,(f + \zeta_\sigma)\,\partial\theta/\partial\sigma$.

\section{Numerical Discretization}

\subsection{Vertical Derivatives}

MPAS-style centred/sided differences. Coordinate-array ordering is
auto-detected (ascending or descending).  Boundary derivatives follow
MPAS \texttt{calc\_vertDeriv\_one}.

\subsection{Sigma Interpolation}

Fields are interpolated from pressure to sigma levels via $\log p$-linear
interpolation.  Values where $\sigma p_s$ falls outside
$[p_{\min}, p_{\max}]$ are filled from the nearest valid level.

\subsection{11-Level Essential Sigma Grid}

The sigma grid uses 11 essential levels spanning the boundary layer
through the lower stratosphere --- all synoptically relevant and all
within ERA5 data range globally ($\sigma_{\min} \cdot \min(p_s) > p_{\min}$):

\begin{equation}
\sigma_k \in \{1.000,\; 0.925,\; 0.850,\; 0.700,\; 0.600,\; 0.500,\;
               0.400,\; 0.300,\; 0.250,\; 0.200,\; 0.100\}
\end{equation}

At sea level ($p_s \approx 101325$\,Pa): $p \approx \sigma_k \cdot 101325$\,Pa.
Terrain-following: $p = \sigma_k \cdot p_s(x,y)$.

This grid deliberately excludes $\sigma < 0.1$ (above $\sim$100\,hPa) ---
mid-stratospheric levels where $\partial\theta/\partial\sigma$ becomes
extremely large, producing unrealistic PV extremes ($\pm 10^5$\,PVU).
The 11-level grid produces PV in the range $[-51, 71]$\,PVU, consistent
with ERA5 native PV $[-62, 111]$\,PVU.
